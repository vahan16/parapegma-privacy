Parapegma

Privacy Policy

Last updated 24 August 2026

Parapegma has no accounts. It never asks for your name, your email address or your phone number, and it has no way to learn them.

  • Your trips are kept on your phone. Deleting the app deletes them.
  • The app cannot read your location. It guesses your home city from your phone’s time zone, and you can change it.
  • Searching sends the shape of the search — cities, dates, nights, how many people — and nothing that names you.
  • Anonymous usage statistics and crash reports are collected, described field by field below.
  • Nothing is sold. There are no adverts and no cross-app tracking.

The rest of this page is the detail behind those five lines.

01Who is responsible

Parapegma is published by Vambran Studio, which is the data controller for the purposes of the UK and EU General Data Protection Regulation. Write to vambranstudio@gmail.com about anything on this page.

02There is no account

There is no sign-up, no password, no email address and no social login. Nothing you do in the app is filed under a person, because the app does not know that a person is there.

The consequence is worth stating plainly rather than burying in section 14: we cannot show you “your data” on request, because there is no “yours” for it to be filed under. That is a result of collecting very little, not a way of avoiding the question.

03What stays on your phone

Held in a database inside the app, readable only by the app, and sent nowhere by us:

  • Your trips — names, dates and cities; each day and each leg, with its operator, service number, departure and arrival times and time zones; fares, including any you corrected yourself; whether you marked something as booked; and any note a suggestion arrived with.
  • Your settings — home city, the city a trip starts from, preferred currency, language, light or dark, and whether you have already seen the first-run tour.
  • The last search you made, so that reopening the app resumes where you left off.

Deleting the app deletes all of it. There is no copy of it on any server of ours, and no way to recover it afterwards.

One honest caveat: your phone’s own backup — iCloud on iOS, Google’s backup on Android — may copy this database along with everything else it backs up. That copy is held by Apple or Google under their terms and controlled by you in your phone’s settings, not by us.

04The app does not use GPS

Parapegma requests no location permission and cannot read your position, coarse or precise. It guesses which city you live in from your phone’s time zone, matched against the cities it already knows. If the guess is wrong, change it in Settings — your choice is kept, and choosing a city to fly into never overwrites it.

05What a search sends

When you search for trips, the app sends one request to our planner, which runs on Cloudflare Workers. It contains:

  • the city or airport you are starting from
  • the departure date, the number of nights, and today’s date
  • how many people are travelling
  • the currency you want prices in
  • what you said matters, as fixed codes from a short list — not free text
  • any cities you named, and the order you put them in
  • whether you want direct flights only, and whether you are returning to the airport you left from

That is the entire request. There is no name, no account and no device identifier in it, because the app holds none to send.

Like every request on the internet it arrives carrying your device’s IP address, which Cloudflare sees and records as part of operating the service and defending it from abuse. We do not store it, and we do not attach it to anything.

The planner asks other services on your behalf, so that your phone does not have to and so that our keys stay off it. Each is asked about places and dates. None of them is told anything about you, and none receives an identifier for you or for your device — they see our server, not your phone.

07Booking links, and how the app is paid

Parapegma sells nothing and takes no payment. When you tap a link to book a flight, a train, a ferry or a ticket, it opens that partner’s own search already filled in with the route, the date and the number of travellers, and we may earn a commission if you book there. It does not change your price, and it never changes how trips are ranked.

Those links carry two things. An affiliate marker, which identifies us and not you. And a short reference of the form placement~tripId~legId — where in the app the tap happened, and which trip and which leg it belonged to. Those two ids are random strings generated on your phone; they mean something to us and nothing to anybody else, and there is nothing about you in either.

From the moment the partner’s page opens, that partner’s privacy policy governs what happens — including any cookies they set and any tracking they do once you are on their site. We have no control over it and no view into it. The partners reachable from the app today are Aviasales, Klook, Tiqets, Hotellook, Booking.com, Ferryhopper, Kiwitaxi and Omio, reached through the Travelpayouts network.

We read a record of bookings back from that network — what was sold, its price, and our commission — so that we know which parts of the app actually pay for it. The network’s own record of a click also includes the IP address and country of whoever made it. Parapegma does not use those fields, does not store them, and never sends them to its analytics.

08Usage statistics

Anonymous, and collected to find out which parts of the app work and which are ignored. They are processed by PostHog, in the United States. What is sent:

  • An anonymous identifier created when the app is installed. It is not your Apple or Google account, not an advertising identifier, and not connected to anything about you. Reinstalling the app produces a new one, unrelated to the old.
  • Which screens you open and which controls you tap.
  • Named events describing the shape of what happened — how many cities, how many nights, how many travellers, how long a search took, whether it found anything, which kind of link was tapped. The rule the code enforces is counts, flags and fixed codes, never the words you typed. Place names you enter are yours and are not sent.
  • Your device and app — manufacturer, model name, operating system and version, app version and build number, language, time zone, and whether it is a simulator.
  • An approximate location — country, region and city — which PostHog derives from the IP address the events arrive from. The IP address itself is not kept on the events.

Session recording is off, and stays off. Nothing you type is captured, no screen is ever recorded, and no profile is built — there is no identity here to build one on.

09Crash reports

When the app crashes, or survives an error worth knowing about, a report goes to Firebase Crashlytics, operated by Google: the stack trace, a short trail of what the app was doing beforehand, the device model, the operating system version, and the app version. Crashlytics generates its own identifier for the installation. No user identifier is ever set — there is none to set. Google keeps crash reports for 90 days.

10Updates

Parapegma updates itself in the background through Expo’s update service. Checking for one sends the platform, the app’s runtime version and the id of the version installed; Expo sees the IP address the check comes from. Nothing about your trips is involved, and an update never uploads anything from your phone.

11What is never collected

  • No name, email address, phone number or postal address.
  • No contacts, photos, calendar, files, microphone or camera. The app asks for none of those permissions.
  • No precise or coarse device location.
  • No advertising identifier. The app carries no advertising SDK, shows no adverts, and does not track you across other apps or websites.
  • No payment details. Parapegma never takes a payment.
  • Nothing is sold, rented, or shared with data brokers — not now and not as a change of plan later.

13Why we are allowed to do this

For anyone in the UK or the EEA, the legal bases under the GDPR are:

  • Running a search you asked for — performing the service you requested by using the app.
  • Usage statistics and crash reports — our legitimate interest in knowing whether the app works and where it breaks, held to the minimum described in sections 8 and 9, with no profiling, no advertising use and no identity attached. You may object to this at the address in section 1.
  • Affiliate attribution — our legitimate interest in being paid for the work, using a reference that identifies a tap rather than a person.

14Your rights

Under the UK and EU GDPR you have the right to ask for access to your personal data, its correction or erasure, restriction of its processing, a portable copy, and to object to processing based on legitimate interests. You can also complain to your data protection authority — in the UK, the Information Commissioner’s Office.

How that works here in practice:

  • Everything the app knows about your travel is on your phone. Delete a trip to erase it, or delete the app to erase all of it at once. You do not need our permission and you do not need to ask.
  • The statistics and crash reports carry no identifier that points back to you, so in most cases we genuinely cannot find a particular person’s records in order to show or delete them. Ask us anyway at the address in section 1 and we will tell you honestly what we can and cannot do.
  • To stop usage statistics being collected at all, delete the app; nothing is sent from a device it is not installed on.

15Children

Parapegma is not directed at children, and is not intended for use by anyone under 13 — or under 16 where local law sets that age. We do not knowingly collect anything from a child. Because there are no accounts, we have no way to verify anybody’s age, which is one more reason the app collects as little as it does.

16Where the data goes

The services in this policy process data in the United States (PostHog, Google and Expo) and on Cloudflare’s global network, which may route a request through any country it operates in. Where personal data leaves the UK or the EEA, those transfers rely on the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses and the UK International Data Transfer Addendum, as incorporated into each provider’s terms.
